\section{Upsampling-factor and POD-rank sensitivity}
\label{app:qr_sensitivity}

This appendix examines the fixed interpolation factor $q$ and POD rank $r$ used by the assisted preprocessors. For PDEs, the $q=1$ column is a POD-only denoising control: the noisy sparse snapshots are projected to rank $r$ and reconstructed at the observed times, but no temporal points are inserted. Values with $q>1$ combine the same POD projection with DMD/EDMD temporal upsampling. The study is not used for oracle selection in the main tables; it is a representative robustness check at the same synthetic-equation level. Unless otherwise stated, the sensitivity run uses relative noise 0.03, ODE sparse factor 16, PDE sparse factor 8, and 5 random seeds. The ODE base step is $\Delta t=0.01$; PDEs use $\Delta t=0.01$, $n_x=64$, $T=2$, and 12 RBF centres. These representative-slice settings need not equal every main-benchmark setting.

Figure~\ref{fig:appendix_q_sensitivity_ode} and Table~\ref{tab:appendix_ode_q_sensitivity} report the full tested interpolation grid, including the no-insertion control $q=1$. They assess the sensitivity of equation recovery to temporal insertion without assuming that the main choice $q=5$ is optimal. The selected value of $q$ is held fixed in the main benchmark rather than chosen using these ground-truth metrics.

The highest-score grid points in this representative slice are Advection--diffusion: $(q,r)=(3,4)$; Burgers: $(q,r)=(1,5)$; Fisher--KPP: $(q,r)=(3,10)$. These descriptive optima use synthetic ground truth and do not define a deployable hyperparameter-selection rule. A default-versus-best comparison may change both rank and interpolation factor. Attribution specifically to temporal insertion therefore requires comparing $q=1$ and $q=5$ at the same rank on the same noisy snapshots; the matched controls retain the fixed main ranks rather than selecting the best point from this grid.
